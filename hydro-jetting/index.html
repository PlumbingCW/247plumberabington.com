Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends high-pressure water through a hose and a nozzle to scrub the inside of a pipe. Where a cable pokes a hole through a clog, a jet washes away the grease, scale and silt that made the clog in the first place. It is the usual answer when a drain keeps clogging, and it is a sensible call when sewage is backing up and a cable has not held.

If water is coming up in the house right now, stop using fixtures and call (215) 714-6692. The line is open 24/7, and a dispatcher can tell you whether the situation points to jetting or to a camera look first.

The snake stopped short, or the clog came right back

The cable went in and stopped

A cable that stops partway down a line, sometimes 50 or 60 feet from the cleanout, has met roots, a heavy buildup or a pipe problem. A jet is not the automatic next step, and a camera should come first.

It drains for a few weeks, then slows again

That rhythm usually means the cable opened a narrow channel and the walls stayed coated. The channel narrows again as new grease and soap collect.

Roots show up every spring

Roots follow moisture to a joint and grow into the pipe. Cutting them gives you months, and removing the buildup they catch can give you longer.

The kitchen line never fully clears

Grease that has cooled and hardened on the pipe walls is a job for hot, high-pressure water rather than a cable.

Slow drains in several places

When more than one fixture slows, the buildup is often in the shared branch or the main, where a jet reaches farther than a hand tool.

Different visits, same answer

If two earlier cable visits each fixed it for a short while, ask for a camera and a jetting quote, not a third cable run.

What the jet does inside the pipe

The hose goes in through a cleanout or an opened trap. The nozzle has jets that point backward, so the water pulls the hose forward through the pipe, and others that spray the walls in a circle as it passes.

Grease, scale and silt are scrubbed from the pipe and carried toward the main, where the flow takes them away. For roots, the plumber may use a cutting nozzle or a cutter on a cable first, then follow with the jet to wash out what is left.

A second camera pass after the jetting shows the cleaned walls and any damage the buildup was hiding. That record helps you decide whether the pipe needs only maintenance or needs a sewer inspection and repair plan.

What to do first when sewage or gray water comes up in the house

Backups are the urgent side of this service. Whatever tool ends up clearing the line, these steps limit the mess in the meantime.

1Stop all water use

No washer, dishwasher, showers or flushing. Water you send down has only one place to go, and it is the opening that is already overflowing.

2Keep people and pets out

Sewage on a basement floor is a health concern. Keep children and pets away, and wear boots and gloves if you have to cross it.

3Mind the electrics

If water is near a panel, a furnace or a floor outlet, leave the power alone and say so on the phone. Do not wade through standing water to reach a breaker.

4Note what is affected

Which drains bubble, which fixtures still work, and whether it started during rain. Those details help us tell a house-line clog from a main line problem.

5Call the right line

Call (215) 714-6692 for a plumber. If neighbors have the same problem, also call Abington Township's sewer backup number, 215-884-8329, since the public main may be the cause. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

When jetting fits and when a camera comes first

Jetting is not the answer to every clog. The deciding factor is the condition of the pipe, which is why the camera runs before the water does.

Jetting usually fits

  • Grease and soap coating the walls of a sound pipe
  • Scale and silt that a cable only partly removes
  • Roots in a joint, once a camera shows the pipe is intact
  • A clog that has returned after cable cleanings
  • Routine maintenance of a line with a known buildup problem

Camera first, jetting may be wrong

  • A cable that stopped and would not go farther
  • Pipe that looks cracked, belly-shaped or offset
  • A line that has collapsed or is partly missing
  • Old pipe with joints that have already separated
  • A backup that began right after digging or heavy loads nearby

From cleanout to final camera pass: how the visit runs

1Find the way in

The plumber locates a cleanout or opens a trap or vent so the hose can reach the line.

2Look before spraying

A sewer camera runs through the pipe so the plumber can see roots, buildup and any damage that would rule jetting out.

3Jet the line

The nozzle goes in and works back toward the cleanout, scouring walls and flushing debris down the line.

4Look again

A second camera pass confirms the walls are clean and shows anything the buildup had covered.

5Talk through the result

You get a plain description of what was found, a photo or video where available and a recommendation on whether to schedule maintenance cleanings or repair work.

Why older Abington lines suit a camera look

About 61 percent of the 23,232 homes in Abington Township predate 1960, with a median build year of 1956, according to Census American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024. Drain and sewer pipes from that era are often cast iron or clay, which can scale up inside or shift at the joints. Houses near Willow Grove and Elkins Park fall in the same age range, so a camera check before jetting is a sensible habit.

The Township also asks residents to keep fats, oil and grease out of the system, and the Township's own name for the problem is FROG, for fats, roots, oil and grease.

Where jetting sits next to the other drain services

For a slow single fixture, drain cleaning with a hand tool or small cable is usually the quicker, cheaper path. For the large pipe that leaves the house, see sewer line cleaning, where jetting is often part of the plan.

Jetting cleans a pipe and does not repair one. If the camera shows a crack, a break or a bellied section, the next step is a repair plan, and we will explain the options before any work begins.
