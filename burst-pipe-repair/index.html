When a burst pipe is an emergency: your first ten minutes

Every burst is different, but the first ten minutes follow the same order in almost every Abington house.

1Shut off the main

Most homes here have the main valve where the water line enters the basement wall, usually near the meter. If the valve will not turn, say so on the phone, because that changes how the plumber plans the job.

2Open the lowest tap

A basement utility sink or an outdoor spigot drains the pipes faster and takes pressure off the break. Leave it open until the plumber says otherwise.

3Keep power away from water

If water is dripping toward a panel, a ceiling light or a floor outlet, do not touch them. Switch off the breaker only if you can reach it without stepping through water.

4Protect what you can

Lift rugs, move electronics and pull furniture off wet floors. Photograph the damage before you clean up, since your insurer will ask for it.

5Turn off the water heater

If the broken pipe is on the hot side or the tank is draining, switch off the heater at the gas control or breaker so it does not run dry.

6Call and stay reachable

Give the dispatcher your address, where the water is coming from and a number that will pick up. A short clear description lets the right plumber arrive with the right parts.

Why pipes fail in Abington homes

The median home in Abington Township was built in 1956, and about 61 percent of the 23,232 homes in the township predate 1960, according to Census American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024. Houses that age often carry galvanized steel supply pipe, early copper with soldered joints, or a mix of both from decades of repairs. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside, so it can look fine on the outside and still split at a threaded joint.

Winter adds a second cause. NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals put the average last spring freeze near April 4 and the first fall freeze near November 7 at the Philadelphia Northeast Airport station. Pipes in unheated garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls face roughly five months of freezing risk.

A frozen pipe does not usually burst where the ice is. It splits where pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet, and the water arrives when the pipe thaws. Homes in Glenside and the older streets around Jenkintown share this housing vintage, so the same advice applies there.

If your house sits in one of the older sections, such as Roslyn, Rydal, Meadowbrook or Ardsley, ask the plumber what material is on either side of the repair. A clean fix on one section can hide a pipe of the same age that will fail next. That conversation is part of a good repiping assessment, and it is worth having before you pay for a second emergency visit.

Signs a pipe has already split

A wet spot that keeps spreading

A ceiling stain or damp patch of drywall that grows over a few hours points to a pressurized leak above it, not a one-time spill.

Water running when everything is off

If the meter dial keeps turning with every fixture closed, water is going somewhere. See the leak detection page for how to read the meter.

A sudden drop in pressure

Weak flow at every tap, or a gurgling sound after a cold night, often means a split on the supply side.

Warm spots on the floor

A warm patch under a bathroom or hallway floor can mean a hot water line is leaking under the surface.

Rust-colored or cloudy water

Discoloration after a pressure drop can mean sediment has been stirred up by a failing section of older pipe.

A hissing or dripping sound in a wall

A steady hiss behind drywall is a pressurized line. A single slow drip may be a joint, but it should still be checked quickly.

How the break gets found without opening every wall

The plumber we send will usually begin with the simplest evidence: the meter, the shutoff and the pressure at a hose bib. If the main is closed and the meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside, and the search narrows to the wet area.

From there the usual tools are a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows temperature differences behind drywall, and a pressure test on an isolated section of line. Acoustic listening equipment helps with slab and buried lines. The goal is one small access hole in the right place rather than a wall opened at random.

Once the break is exposed, the plumber will show you the damaged section and explain the options before any cutting begins. Photos of the failed pipe are useful for your insurer and for your own records.

Repair options after a burst

The right fix depends on what failed and what the pipe around it looks like. These are the common paths.

What the plumber findsUsual repairTrade-off
A single split in sound copperCut out the damaged section and sweat in a new piece with couplingsFast and permanent, as long as the neighboring pipe is healthy
A frost split in a short runReplace the run with new copper or PEX and insulate itInsulation or relocation is what keeps it from happening again
Galvanized pipe corroded at the jointReplace the failed section, or the whole branchPatching one joint often leads to another failure within a short time
Several leaks along one lineReplace the line with a new run, see repipingHigher cost today, fewer emergency calls later
Break on the line from the streetRepair or replace the customer-owned portion, see water service line repairOwnership is split with the water utility, so the break location matters

What you can do yourself and what to leave to a plumber

Safe to do while you wait

  • Close the main water valve
  • Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines
  • Move belongings out of the water
  • Take photos for the insurer
  • Mop standing water away from outlets

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Cutting out or replacing pipe
  • Soldering or pressing new fittings
  • Working on a gas-fired water heater
  • Anything involving the main line from the street
  • Deciding whether a line needs full replacement

Who owns the pipe from the street?

Aqua Pennsylvania supplies public water to most of Abington Township. Aqua's published guidance says the company owns the line from its main to the curb, and the customer owns and maintains the portion from the curb stop to the building. If the break is outside, finding which side of the curb stop it sits on decides who fixes it. Aqua's customer service line is 877-987-2782 for questions about its side.
