How a Gas Water Heater Install Works in Abington, PA

A gas water heater swap is a one-visit job for a licensed plumber in many homes, but the gas line and the vent are where it can go wrong. Here is how the work goes, what to ask, and when to walk out of the house first.

By Abington 24/7 Plumbing | Published 2026-10-22

A gas water heater install has six parts: shut off and drain the old tank, set the new one, connect the water lines, connect the gas line and the vent, light it and test it, then walk the owner through the settings. A licensed plumber should do the gas and vent work, because mistakes there can cause gas leaks or carbon monoxide. Many like-for-like swaps finish in one visit, though the time depends on the house.

If you smell gas at any point, do not wait for a plumber. Leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Planning a swap ahead of time? Our water heater installation page covers what a plumber checks on site, and (215) 714-6692 reaches the dispatch line at any hour.

Gas smell? Leave first, call second

Natural gas usually has a rotten-egg odor added so leaks are easier to notice. If you smell it near the water heater, do not flip a light switch or hunt for the leak. Walk out, take everyone with you, and use a cell phone or a neighbor's phone once you are outside.

Call the gas utility's emergency line or 911 before you call a plumber. The township lists PECO as the gas provider in Abington, so find the emergency number on your PECO bill or on peco.com today and save it in your phone. Call (215) 714-6692 after the gas company has cleared the house, so a plumber can repair or replace whatever failed.

What happens during a gas water heater install

Every plumber has a slightly different routine, but this order is typical for a like-for-like swap in an older Abington basement.

1Agree on the scope

The plumber checks the old tank's size, the vent route and the gas connection, then explains what stays and what changes. Ask for the scope in writing so a new vent or gas fitting is not a surprise later.

2Shut off the gas and water

The gas valve at the heater is closed, the cold supply is shut, and the tank is drained through the bottom valve to a floor drain or a hose run outside. An old tank often sends out a lot of sediment, so draining takes patience.

3Disconnect and remove the old tank

The gas connector, vent, water lines and relief valve pipe come off. Even a drained tank is heavy and awkward on basement stairs, which is why a hand truck and two people are common.

4Set the new tank and connect the water

The new heater sits level, often in a drain pan. New connectors go on the hot and cold lines, along with a shutoff valve and a temperature and pressure relief valve piped down toward the floor. An expansion tank may be added, depending on the plumbing setup.

5Connect the gas line and the vent

A new gas connector and shutoff go on, and every joint is checked with leak-detecting solution, never a flame. The vent pipe is sized to the heater's manual, rises steadily and has tight joints.

6Fill, light and test

The tank fills with a hot tap open to push the air out, then the pilot or igniter is lit. The plumber watches the burner flame, confirms the exhaust is drawing outdoors, and checks every gas joint a second time.

7Inspection and walk-through

If the job goes through the township, the registered master plumber schedules the inspection. You should leave with the temperature setting, the location of the shutoffs and the manual in your hand.

Vent types and what each one asks of your house

The vent decides more of the job than the tank does. A change in vent type is the most common reason a quote grows, since a new vent adds both parts and labor.

Vent setupHow exhaust leavesWhat to know
Atmospheric (natural draft)Rises through a metal vent pipe into a chimney or flueCommon in older homes. Needs a sound flue and a steady upward run. A cracked or oversized chimney often needs a liner.
Power ventA fan pushes exhaust through plastic pipe out a side wall or roofNeeds an electrical outlet nearby and a clear spot outside. The fan is one more part that can wear out.
Direct ventA sealed system with one pipe for exhaust and one for outside airDoes not use room air, which suits a tight utility closet. Needs two pipe runs to the outdoors.
CondensingCool exhaust leaves through plastic pipe and a condensate drain is neededHigher efficiency and higher cost up front. Condensing tankless units follow the same rule, see tankless water heaters.

Follow the vent sizes and clearances in the manufacturer's manual and the code the township enforces. Your plumber should name which one applies to your unit.

What you can handle and what belongs to a licensed plumber

Gas work is not a place to learn on the job. The split below keeps the risky parts with someone trained and insured.

Reasonable to do before the visit

  • Clear a path and a work space around the old tank
  • Take a photo of the nameplate and of the vent
  • Find the cold water shutoff and the gas shutoff
  • Look outside for snow, ice, nests or debris at the vent
  • Write down your questions about the quote

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Cutting, threading or connecting gas pipe
  • Lighting the burner and checking the flame
  • Sizing and routing the vent
  • Setting the relief valve and its discharge pipe
  • Handling the township permit and inspection

Questions worth asking before you approve a quote

A good quote answers these without being pushed. If one answer is vague, ask for it in writing.

  • ✓Is the vent staying or changing? Reusing a sound vent keeps the job simple. A new vent adds parts, labor and sometimes a wall or roof opening.
  • ✓Are the gas connector and shutoff new? Old flexible connectors and stiff shutoff valves are cheap to replace while the tank is out.
  • ✓Is an expansion tank part of the job? Ask whether your plumbing setup calls for one, and why. Do not assume it is required by the township.
  • ✓Who handles the township side? The permit and inspection go through a registered master plumber, so ask who that is.
  • ✓What happens to the old tank? Most plumbers haul it away. Confirm that before the day of the install.
  • ✓What is the price before work starts? You should get a clear price before work starts, and any change in scope should be explained before it is added.

Why one install takes longer than another

A swap in the same spot with the same vent is often a few hours of work, while a move across the basement or a new vent route can stretch into a second visit. The things that slow a job are usually access, a damaged flue, old gas piping that needs replacing, and a tank that will not drain cleanly. A plumber can only give a real estimate after seeing the setup.
