What Drives the Cost to Replace a 50-Gallon Water Heater in Abington, PA

No honest plumber can name a final number for your house over the phone. This guide shows what moves the price so you can read any quote and spot what is missing.

By Abington 24/7 Plumbing | Published 2026-10-22

Call 24/7
(215) 714-6692
Call now

The cost to replace a 50-gallon tank water heater depends mostly on five things: the fuel (gas or electric), the venting, the tank you choose, how hard the old unit is to remove and reach, and the extras the job needs. A like-for-like swap in an open basement is the simple end. A change of fuel, a new vent or a tight closet pushes the quote up.

We do not quote numbers in a blog post, because the real price depends on what the plumber finds at your house.

What you can do is learn what belongs on the quote. That way two bids can be compared line by line instead of by the bottom total.

The five things that move the price

A fair quote is built from these pieces. Each one can raise or lower the total on its own.

Gas or electricA gas unit needs a flue and a gas connection. An electric unit needs a circuit that can carry the load. Switching from one to the other adds work, and usually adds cost.
Venting and the chimneyA gas heater must vent safely. An old flue may need a liner, a new connector or a different route, and that work is often the biggest surprise on a quote.
The tank itselfWarranty length, burner type, insulation and the efficiency rating all affect the unit price. A longer warranty usually costs more up front.
Access and removalA heater in an open basement is quick to swap. One in a closet, an attic or behind a finished wall takes more time and sometimes more hands.
Add-ons and code itemsNew shutoff valves, a drain pan, a discharge line for the relief valve and an expansion tank each add parts and labor. Ask which of these your job needs and why.

Some of these items may already be in good shape, and some may not be. Call (215) 714-6692 and describe your heater, and a dispatcher can tell you what details to have ready for a clear price before work starts.

Abington permit note

Permits and plumbers in Abington Township

Abington permit note

In Abington, plumbing permit applications must be submitted by a master plumber registered with the township, and that plumber schedules the inspections. A homeowner does not pull the permit as the township posts its rules. That is worth knowing because the plumber you hire needs to hold that registration if your job needs a permit at all.

Whether a water heater swap needs a permit is not stated plainly on the township's forms page or in its list of permit-required residential work. Ask Code Enforcement at the Township Building on Old York Road, and ask your plumber the same question. Do not treat a vague answer as final, and do not assume an expansion tank or a relief valve discharge line is or is not required. Ask the plumber to name the rule that applies.

Pennsylvania also keeps no statewide license for most construction contractors. Home improvement contractors who do at least $5,000 of work a year must register with the Attorney General, and you can look a contractor up at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov.

Situations that change the job

The old tank is leaking now

A tank that has failed will not be repaired, and water on the floor is urgent. See the water heater replacement page for the repair or replace question.

Switching gas to electric or the reverse

A change of fuel brings gas piping or electrical work, plus new venting questions. Plan for a longer visit and a longer quote.

The heater sits in a finished space

A closet, a furnace room or a crawl space slows removal. Tell the dispatcher about stairs, tight turns and doors.

A bigger or smaller tank is on your mind

A different size can change the fittings and the space needed. Ask whether fifty gallons still fits how many people use hot water.

The old unit has no drain pan

In a finished area a pan and drain line protect the floor below. It is a modest part that can save you from a repeat of the original problem.

Questions to settle before you approve a quote

A short list of questions beats a long comparison chart. Bring these to the first call.

Ask about the heater and the install

  • Is the venting reused or rebuilt
  • Is the same size and fuel being kept
  • What is the warranty on the tank and on the labor
  • Who handles the permit, if one applies
  • What happens to the old tank

Be cautious if you hear this

  • A single total with no breakdown
  • No mention of venting on a gas unit
  • A price that depends on skipping the permit
  • Pressure to decide before the visit is over
  • No answer on who does the work

Why a slow leak can be the costly one

Aqua Pennsylvania's guidance says a leak inside the home is the owner's responsibility, and its own page states that Aqua does not offer leak adjustments in Pennsylvania. That means a tank that drips for weeks can add water bills and floor damage on top of the heater itself. If you see rust at the base or a damp floor, call before it grows. Our water heater repair page explains what can be fixed and what cannot.

How the quote changes with the age of the house

Census estimates for 2020 to 2024 put the median Abington home at 1956, and about 61 percent of its 23,232 homes predate 1960. Houses that old often have a heater in a basement corner with a flue that was built for an older unit. That makes the venting line the one most worth asking about.

A planned swap also costs less stress than an emergency one. If your heater is old and starting to rust, call during the day so the plumber can inspect the flue and the connections.
