Toilet repair covers everything from a worn fill valve to a cracked bowl, and installation means setting a new fixture on a sound flange with a tight seal. Most calls start with one of the few causes in the table below.

Toilet overflowing onto the bathroom floor: the first two minutes

These actions work on almost any residential toilet, and none of them needs a tool.

1Close the supply valve

It is the small oval or round handle on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise by hand. If it will not move, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher so.

2Stop the flapper

Lift the tank lid and push the rubber flapper down over the drain hole at the bottom of the tank. Holding it closed keeps tank water out of the bowl.

3Hold off on the plunger

Plunging a bowl that is already full to the rim splashes water over the edge. Wait until the level drops, or let the plumber handle it.

4Watch the other drains

If the tub gurgles or the sink backs up when the toilet struggles, the clog is past the toilet. Stop all water use and say so on the phone.

5Protect the floor and the power

Lay towels at the base, lift rugs, and keep power strips and cords out of the water. A wet subfloor is far worse than a wet bowl.

6Call and stay reachable

Give the address, say whether water is still rising, and leave a number that will pick up. That lets the right plumber arrive with the right parts.

Wax rings, flanges and the floor under the toilet

The wax ring is a ring of wax, or a wax-free gasket, that seals the toilet outlet to the closet flange. When it fails, water seeps out at the base after a flush, the floor feels soft or stained, or a sewer smell appears. Even a slight rock breaks the seal, so a loose toilet often becomes a leaking one.

The flange is the part people forget. It bolts to the floor and holds the toilet in place, and it may be cast iron, brass, PVC or plastic. A flange that is cracked, rusted at the bolt slots or sitting below the finished floor will not hold a new seal, and the plumber may add a repair ring or an extension.

Abington's homes are old. The median house was built in 1956 and about 61 percent predate 1960, according to Census ACS estimates for 2020 to 2024. In a house that age, the flange and the pipe beneath it may be original cast iron, and the floor around a toilet that has leaked for months may be soft. Ask the plumber to press on the subfloor before the new toilet goes down.

A toilet that runs or leaks without overflowing

A toilet that hisses or refills on its own is usually losing water past the flapper into the bowl, and the fill valve keeps topping up the tank. You can test it by putting a few drops of food coloring in the tank and waiting 15 minutes, since color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. Flappers, chains and fill valves are inexpensive parts, and many people replace them with a screwdriver and a wrench.

Call a plumber when the tank is cracked, when the shutoff valve will not close, or when the leak is at the base rather than the tank. A running toilet can waste a lot of water over a day, and Aqua's water-leak guidance suggests reading your meter across a two-hour window with no use to confirm a hidden leak. Our leak detection page explains how that check works.

Putting in a new toilet: what the plumber checks first

A replacement toilet only works if it fits the room and the pipe beneath it. These checks happen before the old unit comes out.

1Rough-in distance

The rough-in is the measurement from the finished wall to the center of the flange. Twelve inches is the most common size, but 10 and 14 inch layouts exist, and a toilet built for one will not sit right on another.

2Flange condition

The plumber looks for cracks, rust and height. A flange that sits too low or too high changes the seal, so it may be raised, repaired or replaced before the toilet is set.

3Supply line and shutoff valve

An old stop valve that has not moved in years may leak when turned, so it is often replaced with a quarter-turn valve and a new braided line. If it will not close, see our faucet and valve repair page.

4Setting and sealing

The new toilet goes down on a fresh seal, level in both directions. The bolts are tightened a little at a time, since overtightening can crack the porcelain.

5Flush test

Several flushes with the base and the line watched closely tell the plumber if the seal and the connections hold. A toilet that cannot wait can often be done same-day when available.

Why Abington Township asks residents to stop flushing wipes

The Township owns and operates the sanitary sewer system and treatment plant on Fitzwatertown Road. Its wastewater guidance lists wipes among the leading causes of blockages and tells residents to stop flushing them, along with the fats, roots, oil and grease it calls FROG. For you this is practical, because a toilet that clogs after wipes is a plumbing job, and a wipe-packed lateral can keep returning until the wipes stop.

When repeated clogging points to the main line

A single clog is a toilet problem. A toilet that clogs again within weeks, a tub that gurgles when the toilet flushes, or a basement floor drain that bubbles all point farther down the pipe. In those cases the plumber clears the line instead of the bowl, and drain cleaning or sewer line cleaning is the right fix.

The Township's own FAQ says that if only your drains are slow, the problem is likely inside your pipes. If several houses on the street are backing up, call the Township's wastewater line at 215-884-8329, which it lists as the emergency number for sewer backups. Either way, call us as well for a plumber on your side of the lateral.
