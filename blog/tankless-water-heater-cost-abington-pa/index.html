Tankless Water Heater Cost in Abington, PA: What Changes the Price

A tankless install costs more than a tank swap for reasons you can see on the quote. Here are the parts of the job that move the total, and what to check before you commit.

By Abington 24/7 Plumbing | Published 2026-10-22

Call 24/7
(215) 714-6692
Call now

A tankless water heater costs more than a tank replacement because the house usually needs more done around it. The unit itself is only part of the price. Gas line size, venting, a condensate drain, water quality and wall space often decide the rest.

A tankless unit does not save money on day one in most homes. It can pay back over time through lower standby loss and a longer expected life, but both depend on your use and your install. We will not quote a number here, since the real one comes from your basement.

Seven parts of the job that move the total

The gas line

A tankless unit burns a lot of gas when it fires. The line to the unit may need to be larger than the one feeding a tank, and that work adds pipe and labor.

The meter and service

The gas meter and the service to the house must carry the added load. Ask the gas utility before the install, not after.

Venting

These units need vent parts listed for that exact model. Moving the vent through a wall or roof is a cost driver in many houses.

Condensate drain

High-efficiency units make acidic water that must drain away. A nearby floor drain is easy. A drain that is far away adds pipe and sometimes a pump.

Electrical needs

Even gas units need power for the fan and controls. An electric tankless unit needs a heavy circuit, which can mean a panel upgrade.

Water quality

Minerals can scale the heat exchanger. A scale filter or a service plan may be worth pricing in. Test your tap first.

Removing the old tank

Taking out a tank, patching old piping and fitting new valves is part of the labor. A basement corner is easy, a closet is not.

Abington gas note

Gas load, PECO and Abington's permit rules

Abington gas note

A Department of Energy Building America guide says a gas tankless unit can add 175,000 Btu or more to a home's gas load, and that the in-house gas line serving it will in most cases need to be three-quarters of an inch in diameter.

It also says the incoming gas capacity should be confirmed, and that vent parts should be listed and tested for the particular unit. That is why a gas tankless quote should include a line sizing check before any price is firm.

Abington Township's residential applications page lists a gas line diagram and a PECO adequacy report or form when gas is run to a structure. Plumbing permits in the township are filed by a master plumber registered with the township. Ask your plumber whether your job triggers either form and who handles the paperwork.

We could not confirm any current PECO tankless rebate, so do not count on one. Ask PECO directly whether the existing meter can carry the load and whether any program applies today. If you ever smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call a plumber.

Source type: U.S. Department of Energy Building America tankless guide, Abington Township residential applications page and township plumbing permit guidance

What to confirm before you sign a tankless quote

Bring this list to the first visit. Each item is a cost driver the quote should name.

  • ✓Gas line sizing The plumber should size the line from the unit's rated input, not guess from the old tank's pipe.
  • ✓Utility capacity Ask PECO whether the meter and service can carry the added load.
  • ✓Vent route Get the exact vent parts, the route and where the exhaust terminates outside.
  • ✓Condensate path Ask where the acidic drain water goes and whether a pump is needed.
  • ✓Water test Test your tap for hardness before you decide on a filter or softener.
  • ✓Permit and forms Ask who files the permit and the gas forms, and who schedules the inspection.
  • ✓Service plan Ask how often the unit should be flushed and what that visit involves.

Hot water that runs out on a cold morning

A tankless unit gives hot water as long as it can heat the flow, but it can struggle when several fixtures run at once. The plumber should ask how many showers and appliances run together. If two bathrooms and a dishwasher overlap in your house, say so before the unit is sized.

Why older Abington houses add a step

Census estimates for 2020 to 2024 put the median Abington home at 1956, with about 61 percent of its 23,232 homes built before 1960. Houses that old often have smaller, older gas piping and a flue built for a tank. Both are good reasons to ask for a gas load check and a venting plan on day one.

The same advice applies in Wyncote and Glenside, where many homes are of a similar age.
