Before anyone digs, one question decides who fixes the pipe. Aqua Pennsylvania owns the water service line from its main in the street to the curb, and the customer owns and maintains it from the curb stop to the building. If water is rushing in your yard or basement now, close your main valve and call (215) 714-6692, because the line is answered around the clock.

The curb stop is a shutoff valve in a small cover near the street, often in the lawn or sidewalk strip. A problem on the street side of it is Aqua's to repair, and one on the house side is yours. A pipe that fails after the meter inside the house is usually a burst pipe and follows a different path.

Signs the leak is on the line from the street

The meter runs with the house shut down

Close every tap, then close the main valve. A meter that keeps moving points to the stretch between the meter and the valve, or to the line outside. The leak detection page walks through the two-hour check.

A wet or unusually green strip of lawn

Water pushing up through the soil in dry weather usually follows the path of the buried pipe.

A soft spot in the driveway or sidewalk

Leaking water can wash soil from beneath paving, leaving a dip or crack above the line.

Wet basement wall where the line enters

Water appearing at the entry sleeve can mean the line has failed just outside the foundation.

The sound of running water near the meter

A hiss or rush at the entry point with every fixture off is a strong clue.

A bill that rises with no change at home

A steady buried leak adds up over weeks because nothing in the house shows it.

When a water line is an emergency: water pouring in tonight

A line leaking into a basement or washing out a yard is a same-day call, and the dispatch line is open after hours. Water pressure on a service line is steady, so the flow will not slow down by itself.

1Close your main valve

It is usually inside the basement wall where the line enters. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2Check the meter and the ground

If the meter still moves, the leak is before the valve. Note where the ground is wet and take photos.

3Do not dig or probe the yard

Buried gas, electric and communication lines may share the area, and digging has its own rules covered further down this page.

4Protect the basement

Move belongings off the floor, keep the sump pump running and stay clear of the electrical panel if water is near it.

5Call Aqua if the wet spot is by the street

Low pressure: when the service line is the cause

Weak flow at every tap, or pressure that fades when two fixtures run, can come from the service line. A leak steals water before it reaches the house, and old galvanized steel narrows from rust until little water passes. A partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator or a clogged fixture can cause the same symptom, so the plumber will rule those out first.

A simple clue is where the weakness shows up. If only one faucet is weak, the cause is probably that fixture or its supply. If every tap is weak, including the outdoor spigot, the problem is more likely at the main valve, the meter or the line itself. Tell the dispatcher which it is, and mention any rust-colored water, since that often goes with aging steel pipe.

A pressure gauge on a hose bib gives the plumber a number to compare against. A reading that drops when other fixtures open often points to a restricted line, while a steady low reading may point toward a regulator or a valve.

Repair or replace the line

The right answer depends on what the pipe is made of, how long it runs and how many problems it has had. A single clean failure is a different job from a line that is worn all the way along.

Trenchless or open trench

A trench replacement digs a path along the line, removes the old pipe and lays a new one. It is straightforward, lets the plumber see the whole pipe and often costs less in materials, but it disturbs lawns, walks and driveways. Trenchless methods, where a plumber has the equipment, use small entry and exit holes and pull or burst a new pipe through the old path.

Trenchless is not always available. It depends on soil, depth, bends, the existing pipe and what sits above the line, so the plumber has to inspect the route first. Ask what each option would disturb, what it would cost relative to the other and whether the new pipe will connect cleanly at the curb stop and at the meter.

Aqua's lead service line program for customers

Aqua Pennsylvania publishes a Customer Lead Service Line Replacement Program fact sheet specific to Pennsylvania. It describes replacing customer-owned lead lines at no direct cost to the customer under an access agreement, with a two-year warranty and a yearly cap that depends on Aqua's budget.

Eligibility, timing and any wait list are Aqua's to decide. Ask Aqua at 877-987-2782 and check its service line inventory at aquawater.com/leadmap before you pay for a replacement. If a leak forces an urgent repair, tell the plumber about the lead question so the old pipe is identified.

Before anyone digs: PA One Call and 811

Pennsylvania asks excavators to contact PA One Call at least three business days before digging begins. The call is free for residential property owners, and utilities mark their buried lines before the work starts.

1Dial 811 or visit pa1call.org

Give the exact address and the area to be dug. Out-of-state callers use 1-800-242-1776.

2Wait for the marks

Painted lines and flags show gas, electric, telephone and other buried lines. Do not dig until they appear.

3Plan around the schedule

A planned replacement fits easily. If the break is urgent, the plumber will explain how the process applies and what can be done in the meantime.

4Ask about the permit

The township's plumbing permit covers installing, replacing or modifying pipes, and the plumber confirms what applies with Community Development.

What decides the price of a service line repair

A line that is thirty feet long and shallow is a very different job from one that is longer, deeper and under a driveway. You get a clear price before work starts, and these factors move it.

Length and depth of the lineMore pipe, more digging and more soil to move all add time. Depth makes it harder to reach.
What sits above the pipeLawn is the easiest to restore. Concrete, pavers, a driveway or a root-filled bed add demolition and repair.
Method chosenTrench work and trenchless methods price differently, and the better fit depends on the route and the soil.
Pipe material and connectionsNew copper or PEX is chosen to match the situation, and the connections at the curb stop and at the meter can add work.

Older Abington lines and the pipe you cannot see

The median home in Abington Township was built in 1956, and about 61 percent of the homes predate 1960, according to Census estimates for 2020 to 2024. Service lines in houses that old can be lead, galvanized steel or early copper, and the material often matches what is found inside the basement. If the first foot of pipe at the entry is steel, ask what is buried outside.

Homeowners near Willow Grove and Jenkintown share this vintage, so the advice carries over. A service line replacement is also a good moment to look at the supply pipe inside, because new copper joined to old galvanized can leave a weak link. The repiping page covers that decision.
