Disposal leaking into the cabinet right now: what to do first

1Cut the power first

Turn the wall switch off, and unplug the unit if it has a cord. If it is hardwired, switch off its breaker as well.

2Keep your hands out

The blades are dull, but the flywheel can still crush a finger if it moves. If a spoon or a bottle cap fell in, reach in with tongs only after the power is off.

3Close the water if it keeps spilling

If water is running out of a pipe or hose and not just from the sink, close the main shutoff and open the lowest faucet. Tell the dispatcher if you cannot.

4Empty the cabinet

Move cleaners and the trash can out, put a bucket under the leak and lay a towel on the cabinet floor. A wet particleboard base swells quickly.

5Take a photo of the leak

A picture of where the water first shows helps the dispatcher decide what parts to bring.

6Call and stay reachable

Give the address, say whether the sink still drains and leave a number that will pick up.

Jam, leak, smell and silence: four problems and their usual fixes

Jam and hum

With the power off, put a hex key in the socket on the underside and turn it back and forth until the flywheel moves freely. Press the red reset button and test with water running.

Leak at the back or the top

A loose dishwasher hose clamp, a worn drain elbow gasket or a bad sink flange seal are common. These are parts and seals, so the unit often stays.

Smell that comes back

Food film builds up on the splash guard, the grinding chamber and the drain arm. Scrubbing the guard helps, and a slow drain is a clue that the line also needs clearing.

No sound at all

Check the wall switch, the plug and the breaker, then press the reset. If it stays silent, the cord or switch may have failed, and the plumber will test them.

What to avoid with a disposal that has stopped

A few habits turn a minor jam into a replacement. Skip these until the plumber has looked.

  • ✓Never reach in Not with a hand, a spoon or a wrench. Free the flywheel from below with the hex key.
  • ✓Do not pour in drain cleaner Chemical cleaners can sit in the unit, damage seals and splash back onto the next person who works on it.
  • ✓Do not keep running it A humming motor that cannot turn will overheat, and repeated resets burn out the winding.
  • ✓Skip fibrous and starchy scraps Celery strings, potato peels, rice and pasta swell and tangle in the line.
  • ✓Wipe pans before washing Abington Township's wastewater guidance asks residents to wipe oil-coated cookware with a paper towel, put it in the trash and use the disposal less.

Reattaching the dishwasher line

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small port on the side of the disposal. If that hose has slipped off, the clamp is loose or the port is cracked, water from the dishwasher will leak at the back of the unit. A new clamp or hose is a quick fix, but a cracked port on the housing means the disposal has to be replaced.

After a new disposal is installed, there is one more step. A knock-out plug in the dishwasher port has to be removed before the hose is attached, or the dishwasher will back up into the sink. The plumber takes it out and fishes the plug from the chamber so it cannot jam the grinder.

Repair it or replace it

A disposal is a small appliance with a limited life, so the question is whether the problem is a part or the whole unit.

Usually worth repairing

  • A jam freed with the hex key
  • A tripped overload or loose plug
  • A loose dishwasher clamp or hose
  • A worn drain elbow or sink flange gasket
  • A unit that hums and then runs normally

Usually time to replace

  • A leak from the bottom or the side of the housing
  • A motor that hums and will not free after a reset
  • Heavy rust inside or outside the unit
  • A cracked dishwasher port or flange
  • A unit that has been repaired more than once recently

Installing a new disposal: electrical and plumbing connections

A new unit has to line up with the sink, the drain and the power. The plumber works through these connections in order.

1Sink flange and mounting

The old unit comes off its mounting ring and the flange is cleaned and resealed with plumber's putty or a gasket. The new mounting assembly then locks the unit under the sink.

2Electrical connection

Most units either plug into a switched outlet under the sink or are wired through a cable clamp into a junction box. A sink with no switched power needs new wiring, and that part is electrician's work.

3Dishwasher port

The knock-out plug comes out if a dishwasher is connected, and the drain hose slips on with a new clamp.

4Drain elbow and trap

The discharge elbow connects to the trap arm. The plumber checks that the trap and the pipe are sound and sloped to drain, since an old trap can crumble when disturbed.

5Test run

With the power on, water runs for a minute and the plumber checks every joint, the dishwasher line and the cabinet floor for drips.

When the disposal is fine and the kitchen drain is the real problem

A disposal that seems to jam, gurgles or sends water up the other sink basin may be working fine. If water backs up in the sink while the dishwasher runs, or the sink drains slowly with the disposal off, the clog is in the pipe past the unit. Replacing a good disposal will not fix that, so the plumber checks the drain first.

A slow kitchen line usually calls for drain cleaning, and grease buildup is a common cause. The Township's FROG guidance (fats, roots, oil and grease) is aimed at exactly this, so scrape plates, wipe pans and treat the disposal as a last step rather than a first one.
