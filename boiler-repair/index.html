Boiler repair means finding out why a boiler that heats water or makes steam is not sending warmth to your radiators, baseboards or floor loops, then fixing that part. In many houses the cause is simple, such as a thermostat setting, a switch turned off, low water pressure or an igniter that will not light. Plumbers ask about those first, so it saves time to check them before you call.

If you smell gas at any point, stop and leave the building before you do anything else. If the checks do not bring the heat back, call (215) 714-6692. The line answers 24/7.

No heat tonight? Check these six things before you call

Work through the list in order. Each check takes a minute or two and needs no tools.

  • ✓Thermostat Set it to heat and raise it about five degrees above the room temperature. Replace the batteries if it uses them. A blank screen points to the thermostat or its power, not the boiler.
  • ✓Power Most boilers have a switch that looks like a light switch near the unit or at the top of the basement stairs. Make sure it is on, then check the breaker. A boiler that is off at either place does nothing at all.
  • ✓Fuel Check that the gas valve at the boiler is open, with the handle running along the pipe. If your system burns oil, look at the tank gauge. An empty tank is a common reason for a cold house.
  • ✓Pilot or igniter Older boilers keep a small pilot flame lit. Newer ones use a spark or hot surface igniter and may show a fault light. If you smell gas, do not try to relight anything and follow the gas safety box below.
  • ✓Pressure gauge A hot water boiler often reads somewhere near 12 to 15 psi when cold, but the label on your unit rules. A reading near zero means water has been lost. A steam boiler has a sight glass instead, and the water level should sit near the middle.
  • ✓Radiator tops Touch a radiator at the top and at the bottom. Hot at the bottom and cold at the top on a hot water system usually means trapped air, which a bleeder key can release. On a steam system, a radiator that stays cold may have a stuck vent.

Steam or hot water: which system is in your basement?

The two systems fail in different ways, so the plumber's first question is which one you have. Compare your boiler and a radiator with the table. A guess is fine, because the plumber confirms what you have on the first visit.

Common boiler faults and what they usually point to

No heat at all

The thermostat, the power switch, the fuel supply or a safety lockout is the usual suspect. Run the six checks above first. If the boiler clicks without lighting or shows a fault light, note the pattern for the plumber.

Banging or knocking pipes

On steam, knocking often means water is pooling in a pipe that should slope back to the boiler. On hot water, banging or ticking can be air in the lines or a pump problem. Pounding that starts suddenly deserves a prompt call.

A relief valve that drips

The valve opens when pressure or temperature runs too high. A drip can come from a worn valve, a failed expansion tank or a fill valve that keeps feeding water. Never cap it or plug its discharge pipe.

Low pressure

A hot water boiler that loses pressure has lost water through a leak, an open bleeder or the relief valve. Refilling once is reasonable if your manual shows how. Repeated refilling means the leak must be found, see leak detection.

Thermostat not calling for heat

The boiler sits idle because it never gets the signal. Dead batteries, a wrong mode or a loose wire are common causes. The thermostat section below covers what to try.

When the thermostat is the problem, not the boiler

A boiler fires only when the thermostat closes a circuit. If the screen is dark or the dial is loose, the boiler never hears the request. Fresh batteries, the right mode and a setting well above room temperature solve many no-heat calls.

Age and wiring are the other causes. Older dial thermostats can drift out of calibration, so the house runs colder than the setting. Some smart thermostats need a common wire that old boiler wiring lacks, which is worth checking before you buy one.

To test, set the thermostat to heat and listen at the boiler for a click or a circulator pump starting within a minute or two. If nothing happens there, tell the plumber what you heard. That one detail separates a thermostat fault from a boiler fault.

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm: leave first

Natural gas is given a rotten-egg smell so leaks get noticed. If you smell it near the boiler, leave the building at once and do not flip switches or use a phone inside. Call your gas utility or 911 from outside, and call the plumber only after that.

A carbon monoxide alarm is a separate emergency. If it sounds, get everyone into fresh air and call 911, and do not silence it and go back in. Keep working alarms near sleeping areas, test them on a schedule and replace them when the label says so.

Where a plumber's boiler work ends and a heating contractor's begins

Plumbers handle many boiler repairs, especially anything on the water side of the system. Burner and combustion work is different, and a heating contractor may be the right call. If your problem falls on that side, we will tell you plainly instead of guessing.

Often handled by a plumber

  • Leaking relief valves and fill valves
  • Expansion tank replacement
  • Circulator pump replacement
  • Leaking radiator valves and heating pipe
  • Air vents and bleeders
  • Thermostat swaps

May need a heating contractor

  • Burner and combustion adjustment
  • Oil burner service
  • Flue and venting faults
  • A cracked heat exchanger or boiler section
  • Control board and gas valve faults
  • Full boiler replacement planning

Older Abington houses and the boilers some of them have

The median home in Abington Township was built in 1956, and about 61 percent of the 23,232 homes predate 1960, according to Census American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024. No data we found says how many of those homes use a boiler, so we do not claim it is common.

Why the first cold snap shows which boilers are weak

NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals put the average last spring freeze near April 4 and the first fall freeze near November 7 at the Philadelphia Northeast Airport station. That freeze window runs about five months, and a boiler often works hardest inside it. A unit that sat idle all summer commonly shows its faults on the first cold night.

A house with no heat in a freeze also puts pipes at risk. Water lines in outside walls and unheated garages can freeze within a day or two once the house cools. If the boiler cannot be fixed quickly, ask the dispatcher about protecting pipes, and see burst pipe repair for what to do if one splits.
