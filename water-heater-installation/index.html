Water heater installation in Abington, PA means choosing the fuel, size and venting, setting the new unit, connecting water and gas or power, and testing it before we leave. Most delays on install day come from one item that nobody decided ahead of time.

Work through these eight points first. When you call (215) 714-6692, we can talk through each one and book a same-day appointment when available or a time that suits your week.

  • ✓Fuel Decide between gas, electric or a heat pump unit. Switching fuels changes the vent, the gas line or the electrical circuit, so settle this before anything else.
  • ✓Size Tank capacity should match how many people shower, run a dishwasher and do laundry at the same hour. A bigger tank than the household needs just holds more hot water that cools off unused.
  • ✓Vent A gas tank needs a flue that carries exhaust outside and slopes the right way. If the old flue is rusted, undersized or tied to an unused chimney, it may have to change.
  • ✓Gas line Check that the gas pipe and shutoff reach the new spot and can feed the new burner. A bigger input rating can mean a larger pipe.
  • ✓Expansion tank If a check valve or pressure reducing valve closes the system, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small expansion tank often handles that, so ask the plumber whether yours needs one.
  • ✓Drain pan A pan with a drain line under the tank limits damage if it ever leaks, which matters most above finished space or on an upper floor.
  • ✓Shutoff valves Old valves that have not moved in years can fail when turned. Replacing them during the swap costs little compared with a flooded basement later.
  • ✓Haul-away A full 40 or 50 gallon tank is heavy and awkward on stairs. Settle who removes the old unit and how it gets out of the house.

Vent and gas safety come before everything else

If you smell gas near a water heater or anywhere else, do not look for the source. Leave the building right away, leave the door open behind you, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not call a plumber first and do not flip switches or use a phone inside.

A weak or blocked vent is the quieter danger. Exhaust from a gas burner can include carbon monoxide, and a flue that is cracked, disconnected or pitched wrong can send it into the room. Soot streaks above the tank, a burner flame that flickers or sags, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounding are all reasons to shut the unit off and call.

On an install, the plumber checks the draw of the flue, the joints and the clearance to anything that can burn. If a gas line has to be moved or enlarged, that work is done and pressure tested by a licensed plumber, never by trial and error.

What an older Abington basement means for the new unit

The median house in Abington Township was built in 1956, and about 61 percent of the township's homes predate 1960, according to Census American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024. A house that age often has a chimney flue sized for an older, hotter burner, and the gas and water lines around the tank may be original. Both are worth a look before you pick a replacement.

That is also why a swap from a gas tank to a heat pump or an electric unit is a bigger decision in these houses than it sounds. An electric unit drops the flue question but adds a heavy circuit, and the panel may or may not have room. Ask the plumber to check the flue and the panel together, so you are not paying to fix one and finding a problem with the other.

What install day looks like once the choices are made

A typical tank swap often takes a few hours, though a new vent, a moved gas line or an electrical circuit can stretch it. Here is the usual order.

1Shut off and drain the old unit

The plumber closes the water and fuel, then drains the tank to a floor drain or hose. Expect the hot water to be off until the new unit is filled and heating.

2Disconnect and remove

Gas, vent, water and discharge lines come off, and the old tank leaves the house on a dolly. This is the part that takes two people.

3Set the pan, valves and new unit

The new shutoffs, drain pan, expansion tank and flexible connectors go in, then the heater is leveled and connected.

4Vent and fuel connections

On a gas unit the flue is connected and checked, the gas joints are tested for leaks and the shutoff is labeled. On an electric unit the circuit and breaker are matched to the nameplate.

5Fill, test and walk you through it

The tank fills before power or flame goes on, so the elements and burner never run dry. The plumber then checks for leaks, sets the temperature and shows you where the shutoffs are.

What moves the price of a water heater installation

We give you a clear price before work starts, and it is built from the items below. Two houses with the same size tank can get very different quotes because of what is around the tank.

Fuel and unit typeA like-for-like swap is the simplest. Switching fuels, or moving to a tankless or heat pump unit, adds fittings, circuits or venting.
Vent and gas line workA new vent adds cost, and so does a larger or longer gas pipe. A clean existing flue and a nearby gas shutoff keep the job short.
Code items and extrasExpansion tank, drain pan, new shutoffs and a proper discharge pipe each add parts and time. They are worth pricing as a group, not one at a time.
Access and haul-awayA basement with steep stairs, or a unit in an attic or closet, takes more labor. Removing the old tank is part of the quote, so confirm it is listed.

What you can prepare and what stays with a plumber

Some of the groundwork is easy for a homeowner and saves time on the day. The connections and anything involving gas or the flue are not.

Good to do ahead of time

  • Clear a path from the stairs to the heater
  • Note the size, fuel and model number on the old label
  • Find your main water shutoff and test that it turns
  • Decide who will be home to approve the final price
  • Take a photo of the area for the plumber

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Disconnecting or reconnecting gas piping
  • Sizing and setting the flue or vent
  • Wiring a new circuit or breaker
  • Choosing and installing the expansion tank
  • Pressure and leak testing before the unit runs
