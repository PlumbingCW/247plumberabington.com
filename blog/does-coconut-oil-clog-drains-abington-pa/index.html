Does Coconut Oil Clog Drains? What Abington, PA Homes Should Know

It is natural and it looks like a liquid in the pan, but your pipes treat it like any other fat.

By Abington 24/7 Plumbing | Published 2026-10-22

Call 24/7
(215) 714-6692
Call now

Yes. Coconut oil is a fat, and it turns solid or semi-solid at around normal room temperature. Poured down a sink, it can cool inside the pipe, coat the walls and trap food bits, soap and hair. Hot water only moves it farther along before it sets.

The better move is to let it cool in the pan or a jar, wipe the pan with a paper towel and put the waste in the trash. If a kitchen drain is already slow, call (215) 714-6692 before the clog becomes a backup.

What to do with coconut oil and what to skip

This is the habit change that does the most for a kitchen drain. It costs nothing and takes seconds.

Do this

  • Wipe oily pans and dishes with a paper towel before washing
  • Let used oil cool and solidify in a jar or can, then put it in the trash
  • Scrape cooled fat into the trash, not the disposal
  • Wash cookware with dish soap after the oil is gone
  • Ask the township what to do with larger amounts of used fat

Skip this

  • Pouring melted oil down the sink and chasing it with hot water
  • Running the disposal to grind fat so it goes down
  • Rinsing greasy pots with cold water that sets the fat
  • Flushing oily wipes or paper towels
  • Relying on harsh chemical openers to dissolve a fat clog

Why a liquid in the pan becomes a plug in the pipe

Coconut oil melts at a temperature in the mid 70s Fahrenheit, which is only a little above a comfortable house. Drain lines run through cooler spots such as basement walls and slab floors, and the water carrying the oil cools quickly. The oil then sets in a pale, waxy layer on the inside of the pipe.

That layer is sticky. Each later wash adds soap scum, coffee grounds and food bits, and the opening narrows over weeks or months. The result is often a sink that drains a little slower each time, then stops all at once.

Oils that stay liquid in the fridge are not safe either, because they mix with food waste and coat pipe walls. Coconut oil is just the easiest to picture, since you can see it turn white in the jar on the counter.

What Abington Township says about cooking oil and grease

Abington local note

The township's wastewater page lists kitchen grease and cooking oil among the common causes of sewer backups, and it asks residents to keep grease out of drains. The township calls its guidance FROG, for fats, roots, oil and grease. Its advice is to wipe oil-coated cookware with a paper towel and put that in the trash before washing, and to use the garbage disposal less.

None of these pages name coconut oil specifically. A fat that sets at room temperature falls under the same warning, so treat it like any other cooking oil.

Township code also bars waste with more than 100 parts per million by weight of fat, oil or grease from the sewer. That limit exists because the township owns and runs the sanitary sewer, and grease that gets past your trap can build up in the main. If you have a larger amount of used fat, ask Abington Public Works at 267-536-1036 what to do with it, and do not pour it down the drain.

If sewage comes back into your fixtures when no one is using water, the township's wastewater page gives 215-884-8329 as its emergency number. If only your own drains back up, the page says the problem is likely in your pipes and a plumber should inspect and clear them.

Common kitchen fats and what they do in a drain

The risk is the same across all of them, but the way they set is different. This table shows what you are likely to see.

Coconut oilTurns solid or semi-solid at about room temperatureCool in a jar, scrape, and put in the trash
Bacon greaseSets into a hard white layerPour into a can, let it harden, and throw it out
Butter and gheeFirms up quickly and clings to pipe wallsWipe the pan with a paper towel first

Signs coconut oil or other fat is already building up

A sink that drains slower each week

A gradual slowdown often points to a coating that has narrowed the pipe. A sudden stop is a different problem.

Gurgling after a dishwasher cycle

Air and water fighting through a narrowed line can make the sink gurgle when the dishwasher drains.

A smell that returns after cleaning

Food trapped on a greasy pipe wall keeps rotting. The smell comes back because the source is still in the pipe.

A clog that returns within weeks

If a cable clears the sink and it clogs again soon after, fat may be left on the pipe walls. A camera can show it.

More than one fixture backing up

If the basement floor drain, laundry sink or tub also gurgles or backs up, the blockage may be in the main line.

If you already poured it down the drain

A single pour is unlikely to cause a clog by itself. A habit is what builds one.

1Stop adding more

Do not run the disposal or add more grease. Wipe pans with a paper towel from now on.

2Flush with plenty of water

Run hot tap water for a minute or two with dish soap. This may move a small amount along, but it does not clear a thick coating.

3Watch the drain for a week

If water starts to linger or gurgle, the oil may have set farther down the line.

4Skip the harsh chemicals

Chemical openers can damage older pipes and do not always cut through a thick fat layer. They also create a hazard for the plumber who comes next.

5Call for a cleaning if it slows

A plumber can clear the line with a cable or a jet. See drain cleaning for the usual methods.

When a fat clog needs a plumber

A plunger and a little patience clear many sink clogs. A fat clog is different, because the cooled grease coats the pipe wall and a cable can punch a hole through it without removing it. The drain looks open for a few weeks, then the narrowing resumes.

That is when hydro jetting can help, since a jet of water scrubs the wall and flushes the film down. A camera inspection first shows how much fat is on the pipe and whether the problem is grease or something else, such as roots or a broken section. A main that keeps backing up may call for sewer line cleaning.

In older homes around Wyncote and Jenkintown, drain lines are often cast iron or clay, and rough interior walls can hold grease more easily than new pipe. Ask your plumber what the pipe is made of and how it looks on camera, so you know whether cleaning is enough or whether a repair is coming.
