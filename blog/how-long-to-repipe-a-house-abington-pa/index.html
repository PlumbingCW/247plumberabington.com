How Long Does It Take to Repipe a House in Abington, PA?

Plan on days, not hours. The exact length depends on how big the house is and how easily the plumber can reach the pipes.

By Abington 24/7 Plumbing | Published 2026-10-22

Call 24/7
(215) 714-6692
Call now

A whole-house repipe usually takes days, not hours, and the exact length depends on the house. A small home with open basement ceilings can often be piped in a few days, while a larger house with finished walls, plaster and several bathrooms can run a week or more. Wall and ceiling patching comes after that and adds its own time.

Think of it in three parts. First comes the walkthrough and permit, then the pipe work itself, then the inspection and repairs to the walls. A good plumber walks the house before quoting and gives you a schedule that says when the water will be off each day.

What stretches or shortens the job

Two houses of the same square footage can take very different amounts of time. These are the factors that move the schedule the most.

FactorUsually fasterUsually slower

These are general patterns, not promises. Your plumber should confirm timing after seeing your house.

How a repipe unfolds, from walkthrough to final patch

Every plumber sequences the work a little differently, but most repipes follow this outline.

1The walkthrough

The plumber traces the existing lines, checks what material they are, finds the main shutoff and decides where new pipe can run. This is also the time to ask about access, patching and which rooms will be disturbed.

2Permit and scheduling

In Abington the permit is pulled by a master plumber registered with the township. Booking the inspection ahead of time keeps the walls from sitting open longer than needed.

3Rough-in

The crew runs new supply lines to each fixture, usually working room by room. Old lines are often left in place and capped, or removed where they are in the way, depending on the house.

4Cutover

This is when the house runs on the new pipes. Water is shut off while the plumber ties the new lines into the main and each fixture, and the length of that shutoff depends on how many connections there are.

5Inspection

The township inspector looks at the work before it is covered. Plan the patching start for after the inspection passes.

6Patch and restore

Holes are closed, drywall is patched and paint matches. Some plumbers handle this, while others coordinate with a drywall or paint contractor, so ask who does it before you sign.

Houses from that period often have galvanized steel supply pipe, early copper or a patchwork of both. Those are the homes most likely to need a repipe conversation.

Abington repipe rules

Abington permits, inspections and the line from the street

Abington repipe rules

In Abington, plumbing permit applications must come from a master plumber registered with the township, and that plumber schedules the inspections. The township code says plumbing work must be inspected and approved before it is covered. For you, a repipe quote should name who pulls the permit and when the inspection happens, and Code Enforcement is at 267-536-1000, extension 4, if you want to confirm.

The pipe from the street is a separate question. Aqua Pennsylvania says it owns the line from its main to the curb stop, and the customer owns the line from the curb stop into the house. Aqua's 2026 update says its inventory of customer-owned service lines in Abington was nearly 75 percent complete, with more than 260 lines flagged for replacement in a project scheduled for 2027.

Aqua says the lead program covers the service line, not the pipes inside your house. So do not hold an interior repipe for Aqua's schedule, but do ask Aqua at 877-987-2782 whether your service line is on its lead inventory.

The township's water conservation code sets flow limits for fixtures on new or altered plumbing under permit, 1.6 gallons per flush for toilets and 3.0 gallons per minute for showerheads and faucets, so buy compliant fixtures if you replace them at the same time.

Living in the house while it happens

Water will be off in stretches

Ask for the plan each morning. Fill a few containers for drinking and flushing, and know where the nearest working bathroom is.

Expect dust and noise

Cutting into walls and ceilings is messy. Cover furniture, seal off doorways with plastic and keep children and pets away from work areas.

Clear the way

Empty closets, move furniture away from walls and clear the path from the basement to every bathroom. Access is the biggest schedule factor you control.

Plan your meals

If the kitchen sink or dishwasher is off, paper plates and a cooler save a lot of frustration.

Know the hot water plan

The water heater is usually off while the new lines are tied in. Ask when hot water returns and whether the plumber will flush the lines when the water is back.

Questions that make a repipe quote clearer

A repipe quote should list what is included, not only a total. Ask these before you agree to anything.

  • ✓Who pulls the permit? In Abington it must be a registered master plumber, and the quote should say so.
  • ✓What pipe material? Ask whether the plan uses PEX or copper and why the plumber chose it for your house.
  • ✓How many days with the water off? Get a plain answer on how long the house will be without water at one stretch.
  • ✓Who patches the walls? Find out whether drywall and paint are included or handled separately.
  • ✓What happens to the old pipe? Ask whether it is removed or left in the walls, and what the house records will show.

Is a repipe the right call, and what comes first?

Not every old house needs a full repipe. A single leak in healthy pipe is a repair, but repeated leaks, rust-colored water or weak flow at several fixtures can point to pipes that are wearing out. Our repiping page explains how a plumber decides, and leak detection can show whether a leak is isolated or one of many.

If a pipe has already split, deal with that first. The burst pipe repair page covers the emergency steps, and the water service line repair page covers the section between the curb and your house.
