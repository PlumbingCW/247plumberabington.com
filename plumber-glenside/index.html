Why Tookany Creek shapes the way Glenside basements flood

The 2017 Montgomery County hazard mitigation plan names Tookany Creek as the primary floodplain in the Glenside and Wyncote area. It also records homes in the 100 to 300 blocks of Keswick Avenue reporting flooding. If you live there, a working sump pump and a tested battery backup matter more than new paint on a basement wall, and our sump pump repair page explains how pumps fail.

On Brookdale Avenue the same plan describes a creek levee and a stormwater pump station. During Tropical Storm Allison in 2001, homes there flooded when a stormwater pump could no longer keep up. Rices Mill Road and Bickley Road also appear in the plan's flood history. A street pump moves street water, so the pump and check valve inside your own basement still carry your part of the load.

On the Abington side, the plan says Baeder Run was channelized in 2001 near Wannamaker and Baeder Roads, and four Wannamaker Road homes still report flooding. The township also bought and demolished 38 flood-prone homes after the 1996 flood, including some on Baeder Road and Madison Avenue. If your block is close to those streets, ask the township about flood maps before you finish a basement.

A state DEP project called Glenside Flood Control Phase II had survey work around North Avenue, Glenside Avenue and Rices Mill Road in summer 2025. Cheltenham Township's notice gave no construction date. Treat it as future relief, not as protection for this season's storms, and look at basement waterproofing options if water keeps returning.

What a 1938 median house tends to hide in its walls

In Census estimates for 2020 to 2024, the median home in the Glenside place was built in 1938. About 58 percent of its housing units date to 1939 or earlier, and roughly 86 percent were built before 1960. Those figures cover both townships together, and they are estimates.

Houses of that age often still have galvanized supply pipe and old cast iron or clay sewer laterals, though every house differs. Abington's 2007 township plan says Glenside's sewers were replaced in the 1990s. That work does not tell you the age of the pipe running from your own foundation, so a sewer camera inspection is the way to find out before a backup does.

If a plumber finds several leaks on one galvanized run, ask about repiping the whole branch rather than patching one joint at a time. It costs more on the day, and it can end a string of repeat calls.

What an older Glenside house tends to need first

Water on the basement floor after rain

Start with the sump pump, its discharge pipe and the float. Abington Township says sump pumps must be piped out of the house and never connected to the sanitary sewer, so ask your plumber where yours discharges.

Sewage or gurgling at the lowest drain

Stop using water and call the line. If the blockage is in your own pipe, sewer line cleaning clears it, and the township or Aqua handles the main.

A leak with no visible source

A meter that keeps moving with every fixture off points to a hidden leak. Aqua's guidance is to read the meter before and after a two-hour window with no water use, and leak detection can find the spot.

Weak pressure or a wet yard near the street

A break between the curb stop and the house is the customer's to repair under Aqua's guidance. See water service line repair, and call 811 before any digging.

Rusty water after a cold snap

Brown water often points to corroding galvanized pipe, though a utility main flush can cause it too. A plumber can tell which by checking water at a tap inside the house.

Before we arrive: your first steps with water in a Glenside basement

The order below keeps people safe and limits damage while the plumber is on the way.

1Shut off the main

The main valve is usually where the water line enters the basement wall near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then tell the dispatcher if it will not close.

2Keep power away from water

Do not wade through standing water to reach a breaker panel or an outlet. If the panel is dry and reachable, switch off the circuits near the wet area, otherwise call the line and wait.

3Stop using drains if sewage is coming up

No flushing, laundry or dishwasher until a plumber clears the line. Then call the sewer owner for your side of the line, the Abington Wastewater Department or Aqua.

4Clear the area

Lift boxes and electronics off the floor and photograph the damage before cleanup. Your insurer will ask for those pictures.

5Leave if you smell gas

Go outside and call 911 or your gas utility before anyone calls a plumber. Do not flip switches on the way out.

6Call the line

Dial (215) 714-6692 with your street, cross street and township if you know it. A clear description helps the right plumber arrive with the right parts.

Telling a main-line backup from a clog in your own pipe

Who owns the blockage?

Abington's township FAQ says that if only your own drains are slow, the problem is likely inside your pipes. If every drain in the house backs up or a neighbor has the same trouble, the main may be involved, and the township or Aqua should hear about it too. Calling both is fine, and the plumber can tell you what the camera shows.
