Sump pump not working? Match what you see to what is likely wrong

Callers describe the same handful of problems. Find yours below, and never reach into a pit while the pump is plugged in.

  • ✓Silent pump, full pit Check the plug and reset the GFCI outlet first, since a tripped outlet is the quickest fix. If power is fine, the float switch may be stuck or the motor may have burned out.
  • ✓Runs nonstop A float jammed against the pit wall, a failed check valve letting water fall back, or a blocked or frozen discharge line can all keep it running. It may also be keeping up with real groundwater.
  • ✓Hums but moves no water A jammed impeller or an air lock in the line can do this. The plumber can clear it or replace the pump if the motor is damaged.
  • ✓Cycles every few seconds Short cycling usually points to a bad check valve, a stuck float or a pit that is too small for the pump.
  • ✓Power is out A standard pump cannot run without electricity. A battery backup or a second pump on its own power supply is what keeps water moving.
  • ✓Loud grinding or rattling Worn bearings, gravel or debris in the pit can make the pump noisy. Noise is a warning, since a pump often fails soon after it starts.

What to do while water rises in the basement and you wait for us

Calls at two in the morning are normal in a storm. The dispatcher tells you whether a same-day visit is open, and these steps help in the meantime.

1Keep clear of electricity

If water touches outlets, a power strip or the panel, do not step into it. Switch off a breaker only from dry ground.

2Check the outlet and the float

Reset a tripped GFCI, and look at the float from above without touching the water. If it sits against the pit wall, tell the dispatcher.

3Lift valuables off the floor

Put boxes, rugs and electronics on shelves or furniture. Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer.

4Move the downspout water away

Make sure each downspout empties well clear of the foundation. A short extension can cut how much water reaches the pit.

5Never hose the pit into a drain

Do not run a temporary hose into a floor drain or toilet. The township prohibits sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can also overload the system.

6Call and describe the pit

Tell us whether the pump is silent, humming or running, how high the water sits and whether you have power. That helps the right parts reach you.

Battery backup, check valve and discharge line

A pump is only as good as the parts around it. Three of them decide whether the pump saves a basement or loses to the first long storm.

A battery backup keeps a second pump or the main pump running when the power fails. Batteries wear out and the system needs testing, so ask the plumber how to check it.

A check valve sits on the discharge pipe and stops water from running back into the pit after each cycle. Without one the pump restarts again and again, which wears it out faster.

The discharge line carries water out of the house and away from the foundation. It should run to a spot where water can drain, not toward a neighbor's wall, and it should not freeze or kink. NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals put the average last spring freeze near April 4 at the Philadelphia Northeast Airport station, so an exposed outdoor end can ice over in early spring storms.

Where Abington floods, and what that means for your pump

The township's plan says flooding is the most common natural hazard in Montgomery County and has been a serious problem in Abington. These are the places it names, each with a practical lesson for a basement.

Crestmont and Roslyn along Sandy Run

The Sandy Run floodplain runs from Roy Chester Park in Willow Grove through Crestmont and Roslyn to the Upper Dublin line. A house near it has less margin, so a battery backup and a check valve are worth pricing before storm season.

Glenside along Tookany Creek

The Tookany (Baeder) Creek floodplain crosses Glenside along the Jenkintown boundary, near Baederwood Park. If you live on that side of Glenside, test the pump after any long soaking rain, not only before a forecast.

Meadow Brook toward Pennypack Creek

The Meadow Brook and Valley Road hook runs toward Pennypack Creek past Lorimer Park. Check where your discharge pipe empties, so pumped water does not run back to your own foundation.

The floods of 1996 and 2001

The township's plan says floods as deep as two to six feet above street level have occurred. A pump handles groundwater and seepage, not a creek leaving its banks, so know your shutoffs and move valuables up early.

38 homes the township bought out

After 1996 the township bought and demolished 38 flood-prone homes on Wanamaker Road, Madison Avenue, Baeder Road, Irvin Road and Hamel Avenue. If your street is near those names, treat the pump as essential equipment and service it each year.

What the township requires of your discharge

Abington Township says sump pumps must be permanently piped out of the house, to the yard, a seepage pit or the storm sewer. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is illegal. The township also asks that downspouts and floor drains stay out of the sanitary sewer to reduce wet weather overloading, and it suggests a check valve on basement drains.

For you, that means a plumber should never tie your pump into a floor drain or a sewer cleanout, even as a quick fix. If your system once drained that way, say so on the call. The correction is part of a safe repair, and it protects the township's sewer from your storm water. Our sewer line repair page covers what happens when that system backs up.

What decides the price of a sump pump job

A repair and a new install are very different jobs. You get a clear price before work starts, and these are the things that move it.

Repair or full replacementSwapping a float or an outlet is small. A new pump, a new pit or a battery backup adds parts and labor.
Pump type and capacityPedestal and submersible pumps differ, and a larger inflow needs a stronger pump or a second one.
The pit and the slabAn existing pit in good shape is quick. Cutting a new pit through a concrete floor takes more time.
The discharge routeA short run to a nearby yard is simple. A long run, a seepage pit or a trench takes more pipe and labor.
