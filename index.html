Why older homes drive most plumbing calls here

Abington Township is mostly an older suburb. The median home was built in 1956, and the 23,232 homes in the township break down by decade as shown below.

Houses from the 1940s and 1950s often still have galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains and original clay or cast iron sewer laterals. Those materials fail from the inside, which is why a slow drain or a rusty tap can be the first sign of a bigger problem. If you own one, ask a plumber to look at the pipe material during any repair, and read about repiping an older house before a small leak turns into a flooded floor.

Neighborhoods across the township we cover

The township is a patchwork of older communities, each built in a different decade. Where your house sits tells us a lot about the pipes inside it.

The township's own plan marks the Sandy Run floodplain through this stretch. Basements here get wet first, so a working sump pump matters.

If water is already on the floor

Do these four things in this order. They take less than five minutes and can save a floor.

1Close the main

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. It is usually on the basement wall facing the street.

2Mind the electricity

Stay out of standing water near outlets and the panel. If the breaker is dry and reachable, switch off the affected circuits.

3Stop the drains

For a backup, stop using sinks, tubs and the washer. Every flush sends more into the line.

4Call with the details

Give the dispatcher the address, what you see and a number that will pick up.
