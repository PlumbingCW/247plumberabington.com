The first question is whether the problem is the main line or a single fixture, because the fix, the tool and the urgency are different.

Sewage coming up in the house right now: what to do first

Sewage in living space is a health issue as well as a plumbing one. These steps keep it contained while help is on the way.

1Stop all water use

Turn off the washer and dishwasher, skip showers and do not flush. Every gallon sent down adds to what is coming back up.

2Keep people out of the affected area

Keep children and pets away from standing water. Wear boots and gloves if you must cross it, and wash well afterward.

3Stay clear of electrics

If water reaches an outlet, a furnace or a panel, do not touch them. Tell the dispatcher so the plumber can plan around it.

4Move what you can

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the basement floor and take photos of the water for your insurer.

5Call the plumber, and the Township if neighbors are backed up

Call (215) 714-6692 first. If the blockage seems to be on the street side, Abington Township takes sewer backup calls at 215-884-8329. After-hours calls are answered, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

What blocks a sewer line: roots, grease, wipes and age

Abington Township's wastewater page lists the three biggest causes in one word, FROG, which stands for fats, roots, oil and grease. Roots find a tiny gap at a joint and grow into the pipe looking for moisture. Grease cools and coats the walls, and wipes and paper towels snag on the rough spots until a plug forms.

The housing stock makes all three more likely. The median home in the township was built in 1956, and about 61 percent of its 23,232 homes predate 1960, per Census American Community Survey estimates for 2020 to 2024. Lines from that era are often clay or cast iron with many joints, and each joint is a place for roots to enter.

If you live in an older part of Jenkintown or Glenside, expect the same pattern. A line with roots or scale that has been cleared more than once is a good candidate for a camera look, because the camera shows whether you need maintenance cleanings or a repair.

Township main or your lateral: who handles what

Abington Township owns the public sewer main and the treatment plant on Fitzwatertown Road. The Township's page says the pipe from your house is connected directly to the main, and that if the blockage is between the house and the main, residents should contact a plumber. Where exactly the Township's responsibility starts is not spelled out in an ordinance we could find, so ask the Township if you are unsure.

Clearing the line, from cleanout to camera

1Locate the cleanout

Most houses have a capped cleanout near the main drain in the basement or at the edge of the foundation. If there is none, a toilet may have to be pulled.

2Cable the blockage

A powered cable with a cutting head goes in first to open the line and chop roots. It tells the plumber how far in the blockage sits.

3Jet if the pipe allows

On a sound pipe, hydro jetting washes the walls and carries out grease and silt. If the cable stopped short, the plumber looks first.

4Run a camera

A sewer camera shows roots, cracks, offsets and bellied sections, and confirms the line is open.

5Explain what was found

You hear whether this was a one-time clog, a root problem that will return or damage that needs sewer line repair.

Backups that only show up in heavy rain

If the basement drain backs up mainly during storms, the cause may be rainwater entering the sewer system, not a blockage in your pipe. The Township's wastewater page notes that sewers take on extra water in wet weather, and it says sump pumps must be piped out of the house and never connected to the sanitary sewer.

The Township's FAQ also suggests a check valve on basement drains. Whether one fits your plumbing is a question for a plumber and the Township, so ask. A failing sump pump can also leave water in the basement without any sewer problem at all.

What to tell the dispatcher when you call

A short, clear description lets the right equipment arrive. Gather these before dialing if you can do it safely.

  • ✓Which drains are affected Name them. A floor drain and a tub that back up together tell us more than a general slow house.
  • ✓When it started Say whether it began after laundry, a heavy rain or a flush. Timing helps separate a clog from a storm problem.
  • ✓Whether neighbors are affected A shared problem on the street points toward the Township's main. A single house points toward your own lateral.
  • ✓What has been tried Mention any plunger, drain chemical or earlier cable visit, so the plumber does not repeat it.
  • ✓Where the cleanout is If you know it, say so. If you have never seen one, tell us that too.

When cleaning is not enough

A line that backs up again within weeks of cleaning, a cable that stops at the same spot every time or a camera view of cracked or bellied pipe all point to repair. Sewer line cleaning opens the pipe and does not fix a broken one, so we will say so before suggesting anything more.
